Vega Router · Evidence-grade AI plumbing

Local when it's good enough. Cloud when it counts. Receipts either way.

  • v1 running daily
  • 24-agent adversarial review of v1 · every finding fixed
  • Open-core planned · fall 2026

The missing middle is a well-known gap: sending each piece of work to the right model — the one on your Mac or the one in the cloud — is still a do-it-yourself project, and almost nothing tells you what your hybrid setup actually costs or whether the cheap answer held up. We hit it ourselves, running local and cloud models side by side on one Mac, and built v1 in a week. It is not an AI guessing the perfect model. It is a policy you can read, a cheap route made safe enough to trust, and evidence for the times it wasn't.

Dispatch Desk · in design · split plan

Split plans: the private part stays home, only the hard part goes out

Dispatch Desk, the native Mac app now in design, is meant to show you the plan before anything runs — what would stay on the Mac, what would go out, and what it would cost — and to run it only on your say-so. Picture a 40-page PDF and a legal-grade memo to write from it: the plan would keep the PDF on the Mac and send out only what the memo needs.

  1. Would stay home The document
  2. Would go out Only what the memo needs
  3. Would come back The memo

Run what you can on hardware you own. Buy the biggest models by the job, for the few jobs that need them — not by the month, for everything.

The thesis behind Vega Router, and behind how Vega builds: keep the durable parts of the work where you control them, and pay for intelligence only where it earns its keep.
